## Check-in kernels 01: The 3090 unlock + identify the kernel challenge

**Found (most important first, max 8 bullets, each with a source):**
- The unlock speeds up tensor math. It does not speed up token generation. LLM decode was "practically unchanged" and prefill gained +14% to +36% (GSMGoTech via tech-insider.org, Oct 25 2026). Decode reads every weight once per token, so memory bandwidth limits it (936 GB/s on a 3090). This is the strongest argument for pooling memory bandwidth across machines.
- What the limit is: on GeForce RTX 20/30/40, tensor-core FP16/BF16/TF32 math with FP32 accumulation runs at half rate. The same die sold as RTX A6000 or 6000 Ada runs it at full rate. Two "speed selector" registers control it: `FEAT_OVR_SM_SPD` at 0x0082381c and `_1` at 0x00823820, with 4-bit fields where bit 3 is the override and 00 means full rate (Zagitta/gpu-unlock, `docs/How Ampere was unlocked.md`).
- How the exploit works: NVIDIA published a debug "Booter" firmware encrypted with a known public test key (index 37). Researchers decrypted it and found a stack overflow in the signed Booter that runs on the SEC2 security processor. A ROP chain (the "V67 payload") writes `0xFFFFFFFF` to the write-protect mask `FEAT_OVR_PLM` (0x00823804), and the host driver then writes full-rate values. There is no VBIOS flash and no driver patch. The unlock runs before the NVIDIA driver loads and must be re-run on every boot (same doc).
- Who did it: Zagitta wrote gpu-unlock for RTX 30 on Linux (repo created Oct 18 2026, 17.8k stars by Nov 7, per the GitHub API). JustAResearcher wrote gpu-unlock-ada for RTX 40, and nlzy wrote turing-unlock for the 2080 Ti. physicsdolphin's nv-uefi-unlocker boots from USB, so it also works on Windows and laptops (TechTimes Oct 28; Tom's Hardware, about Nov 1).
- Measured gains are all from owners or developers, with no independent lab. RTX 3090 BF16 went from 77.5 to 129.6 TFLOPS (+67%). RTX 3090 Ti went from 87.5 to 157.4 (+80%). RTX 4080 BF16 gained 1.91x, RTX 4090 FP16 gained 1.45x (power-limited), and an RTX 4060 Laptop gained 1.65x. ComfyUI BF16 ran +47% to +59% faster, which matches the r/StableDiffusion "59%" lead. A 3090 capped at 225 W gained only +25%. RTX 40 real workloads gained +12% to +34% (Tom's Hardware table; TechTimes).
- Risks: the unlock voids the warranty, and turing-unlock warns it "may damage your hardware." It needs Secure Boot off, so games with anti-cheat that requires Secure Boot will not run. Laptop power management can silently undo it. It does not support RTX 50 or datacenter GPUs. NVIDIA has made no public statement in any source I read. I found no legal analysis; DMCA and EULA exposure is an open question.
- Kernel challenge, most likely candidate: the GPU MODE leaderboard competitions that companies sponsor. These include the AMD $100K "Inference Sprint" (Apr 2025), AMD's second competition (Nov 2025), the NVIDIA NVFP4 Blackwell competition with Sesterce and Dell (Nov 2025), and the AMD "E2E Model Speedrun" on MI355X (2026). NVIDIA's "cuda.compute" blog post (Jan 20 2026) is about this leaderboard (gpumode.com/news).
- Other candidates: NVIDIA SOL-ExecBench ranks B200 kernels by a "SOL Score" against the theoretical roofline, on a public leaderboard (research.nvidia.com/benchmarks/sol-execbench). The MLSys 2026 FlashInfer AI Kernel Generation Contest has an NVIDIA track for B200 kernels written by hand or by AI agents (mlsys26.flashinfer.ai).

**Surprises / things that change the plan:**
- None of the demo machines can use the unlock (Macs plus a Dell with no discrete GPU). For customers with old GeForce cards, it speeds up only prefill and image generation, not chat tokens per second. I recommend we do not ship it: a firmware exploit in a clinic or law office undermines our trust story.
- All of the kernel challenges target datacenter GPUs (B200, MI355X). Their ideas transfer to our pool (fusion, quantized kernels, roofline scoring), but their code does not. The "SOL Score" idea, meaning percent of theoretical peak, is a good way to measure our own pool: tokens/sec as a fraction of each machine's memory-bandwidth limit.
